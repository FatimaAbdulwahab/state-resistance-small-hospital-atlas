Read with care. CDC's figures come from healthcare-associated infections (central line bloodstream infections, catheter urinary tract infections, and surgical site infections) that hospitals report to the National Healthcare Safety Network. They are not all clinical isolates, and reporting varies by state. CDC cautions against drawing conclusions about change over time or about one state compared with the nation. Hospital counts include active short-term acute care and critical access hospitals. "Rural" is the CMS flag, which marks about 1 in 5 critical access hospitals as urban; the "small and rural, or critical access" measure counts all of them. short-term hospitals listing 2 or fewer certified beds are left out (in 2020 to 2022 these were mostly temporary COVID-era enrollments of surgery centers and freestanding emergency rooms).
